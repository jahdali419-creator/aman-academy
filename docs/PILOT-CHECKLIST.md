# Aman Academy: 14-day verification checklist

Prepared 12 November 2026. Proposed window: Sunday 15 November to Saturday 28 November 2026 (ten working days, Sunday to Thursday). Decision meeting: Thursday 26 November.

Purpose: establish whether the business can legally operate, deliver training that employers will pay for, and make money. Nothing here is legal advice. Every regulatory point must be closed with a written answer from the authority or a licensed Saudi adviser.

---

## 1. What is known today

### Confirmed from official sources

| # | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | TVTC does not issue a training licence for e-training only. "The system does not allow e-training only." A training licence must be created and its programmes activated by TVTC; e-training approval is then requested from the National eLearning Center. | [TVTC FAQ](https://tvtc.gov.sa/en/ContactUS/FAQs/Pages/default.aspx) |
| C2 | Online training is allowed "for accredited development courses for training facilities that have a training license from TVTC", and also requires a licence from the National eLearning Center. | TVTC FAQ |
| C3 | TVTC licensing has two stages: create the licence, then activate the programmes. Training may start only after TVTC accredits the programmes. The licence is valid five years. | TVTC FAQ |
| C4 | Health courses are "under the supervision of the Commission for Health Specialties", not TVTC. First aid and CPR are likely to fall here (to confirm). | TVTC FAQ |
| C5 | TVTC does not license training delivered through social media apps. | TVTC FAQ |
| C6 | From May 2026, private training providers obtain e-training programme licences from TVTC after completing technical integration with the National eLearning Platform (FutureX). NELC keeps oversight. | [NELC announcement, 25 March 2026](https://nelc.gov.sa/en/media-center/news/unified-e-learning-licensing-private-sector-en) |
| C7 | VAT registration is mandatory above SAR 375,000 revenue in twelve months and voluntary from SAR 187,500. | [ZATCA SME guideline](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/%28SMEs%29%20Guidelines%20%E2%80%93%20Zatca%201.pdf) |
| C8 | The Ministry of Commerce reserves a trade name for 60 days pending commercial registration, and refuses names similar to registered ones. | [Ministry of Commerce e-service](https://mc.gov.sa/en/eservices/Pages/ServiceDetails.aspx?sID=1) |

**What C1 to C6 mean for the plan (my reading, not a regulator's):** an online-only academy with no TVTC-licensed facility behind it does not appear to be a permitted model, even for knowledge-only courses. The realistic routes are (a) obtain a TVTC facility licence, then programme activation, then FutureX integration, or (b) deliver the pilot through an already licensed training facility as its partner. Whether route (b) is allowed, and on what terms, is question T3 below.

### Not found at all (open)

- Any published Civil Defense procedure for approving private fire-safety training providers.
- Any national approval scheme for defensive-driving course providers. Saudi Aramco appears to run its own driver programme in-house; what its contractors must hold is unverified.
- Personal Data Protection Law obligations for a platform holding learner and employer data (SDAIA).
- Whether unlicensed "awareness sessions" with no certificate, or in-house training sold to a single employer, fall outside TVTC licensing.

## 2. Before taking any customer payment

All of these must be closed with written evidence. None can be deferred.

- [ ] P1. Written confirmation of the licensing route for the pilot course: own TVTC licence, or a named licensed partner whose licence covers the programme and online delivery.
- [ ] P2. The pilot programme is activated or accredited by the body that governs it (TVTC, SCFHS, or other as confirmed).
- [ ] P3. E-training approval in place for whoever is the licensed provider (FutureX integration and programme licence).
- [ ] P4. NCOSH regulation read in full and its applicability to the pilot course confirmed in writing.
- [ ] P5. Commercial registration that includes training activity; trade name reserved; national address; bank account.
- [ ] P6. Certificate wording reviewed by the licensing adviser. It states what was attended and assessed, names the licensed provider if a partner is used, and claims nothing else. QR verifies the academy's own record only.
- [ ] P7. Instructor holds the qualification the governing body requires, with a copy on file.
- [ ] P8. Payment provider onboarding requirements known and met; refund and cancellation terms published; VAT position decided with an accountant.
- [ ] P9. Privacy notice and data handling checked against the Personal Data Protection Law.
- [ ] P10. At least one employer has confirmed in writing what the pilot certificate does and does not satisfy for them.

## 5. Customer interview script

Target: the person who signs for training, or the HSE manager who specifies it. Thirty minutes. Do not pitch until question 9.

1. Which safety trainings did you buy in the last twelve months, for how many people?
2. For each, what drives the purchase: a regulation, a client or contractor requirement, insurance, internal policy?
3. Which certificates or providers do you accept today for first aid, fire safety, defensive driving and general HSE induction? Who decides that list?
4. Has an inspector, client or auditor ever rejected a certificate? Why?
5. Do you accept any online-only training? For which subjects? What would you need to see?
6. What do you pay per person per course today, and what is the annual budget? Who approves it?
7. How do you buy: purchase order, annual contract, tender, vendor registration? How long does onboarding a new vendor take?
8. What frustrates you about current providers?
9. If a new provider offered [pilot course] in Arabic and English with a verifiable record, what would it need for you to place an order for ten seats?
10. Would you issue a purchase order or a signed letter of intent for a pilot? If not, what is missing?

Record as evidence: named accepted certificates, a price paid, a volume, the approval path. "Sounds good" counts as zero. A letter of intent or purchase order is the only signal that counts toward Go.

## 7. First-course options to score on Day 9

| Option | Legal route (to confirm) | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| A. Knowledge-based HSE awareness, own TVTC licence | TVTC facility and programme, e-training licence, possibly NCOSH | Time and cost of a facility licence before any revenue |
| B. Knowledge-based HSE awareness through a licensed partner | Partner's licence, if T3 allows | Margin shared; dependence on partner |
| C. First aid and CPR through an accredited partner | SCFHS, SHA or SRCA route; practical session required | Cannot be online-only; instructor supply |
| D. Fire safety or defensive driving through a practical partner | Civil Defense, NCOSH, traffic: all unverified | No confirmed approval path yet |

**Provisional recommendation, to be confirmed or overturned by the Day 1 to 8 answers:** Option B. It is the only one that could produce a legitimate paid pilot quickly without claiming anything the academy does not hold. This is my judgement from the confirmed facts above, and it fails if TVTC says the partner route is not allowed.

## 8. Go / no-go on Day 10

**Go** only if all are true:
- A lawful route for the pilot course is confirmed in writing (P1 to P4).
- At least two of five employers name a real purchasing requirement the course meets, and at least one gives a purchase order or letter of intent.
- The model breaks even at a class size the instructor and platform can deliver, using quoted costs for instructor, payment and licensing or partner share.
- Cash needed before first revenue is an amount the founder is prepared to lose.

**Adjust** (extend two weeks, narrow the course) if the legal route is confirmed but demand or margin is not.

**No-go for that course** if the authority's answer is no, is still missing, or requires a facility and budget the founder will not commit. A missing answer blocks; it does not default to yes.

## 9. Minimum for a legitimate pilot

- One course, one licensed route, one qualified instructor, one or two employer customers.
- Accurate certificate wording, no accreditation or competence claims, verification of the academy's own record.
- Published terms, refund policy and privacy notice; commercial registration and tax position in order.
- Real payments only through an onboarded provider, or by invoice and bank transfer to the registered company.
- Attendance and assessment records kept for every learner.

## 10. Can be developed later

Additional courses; wallet cards; production payment integration beyond invoicing; brand rollout and trademark filing; company dashboard extensions; mobile app; marketing site redesign.

## 11. Out of scope for these 14 days

- Any new logo, font, certificate, card or interface concept.
- Printing or issuing wallet cards.
- Launching all three original courses together.
- Integrating a live payment gateway.
- Any claim of government recognition, accreditation, licensing or professional competence.
- Finalising the public brand name before the trade name and trademark checks.
- Paid advertising.
